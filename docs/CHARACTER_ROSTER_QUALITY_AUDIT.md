## 1. What every fighter has

| Layer | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| Body | one exported body per identity: `public/models/characters/roster/<slug>.glb` (or `kaze/kaze.glb`), 19-bone armature, 17 clips, authored UVs, sRGB PBR materials, ~450–660 KB |
| Mount path | prefab body stands in while the export streams, the export replaces it, the eleven canonical joint names are bound onto its real bones, the signature weapon re-mounts on the new body, generated surface detail is applied |
| Skeleton gate | root tilt, uniform scale, drawable meshes, height, upright silhouette, feet on the origin, head above pelvis, every joint inside the body, all eleven canonical bones resolved |
| Movement | per-character locomotion profile: top speeds (walk/run/sprint), acceleration, braking, air control, jump impulse, gravity and reversal commitment (see the matrix) |

| Transformation | cost, duration, drain, cooldown, stat and move-variant changes, an aura, and material writes on the fighter's own tagged surfaces with exact restore |
| Ultimate | separate meter earned from damage dealt/taken, one spend at activation, refunded if interrupted before the first hit, a named variant while transformed |
| VFX / audio | shared impact/dash/landing effects, projectiles and auras, synthesised Web Audio events |

The `Bones · clips · triangles · KB` column is measured per body by
`node scripts/measure-roster-bodies.mjs` (recorded in `artifacts/roster-bodies.json`), which parses
each GLB with the same three.js `GLTFLoader` the game loads it with; a `?` means that body could not
be measured. The same artifact records meshes, materials, vertices, clip names and bounds per body.

34/34 bodies were measured; every one binds all eleven canonical bones, the largest feet offset from the origin is 0 m, and every launch body is authored by `assets/blender/build_roster_fighters.py` on the same geometry kernel as the reference fighter Kaze with a 19-bone armature and 8 clips. Mounted behaviour (the right body on screen, height and foot alignment, the bound joint names) is asserted by `npm run test:roster-models` and `bash scripts/run-browser-test.sh scripts/audit-roster.mjs`.

These are judgements, scored against the evidence quoted for each dimension. The baseline applies
to every fighter because the pipeline is shared; the exceptions column lists the identity-specific
reasons a fighter scores differently. The honest headline is that the roster is *technically*
uniform and *visually* differentiated only by garment layers, palettes and equipment - the
animation and material axes are what keep it away from Xenoverse/Injustice presentation.

## 4. Status labels

Every launch fighter is **playable and visually improved** within the current project scope: each
mounts its own exported, skinned, animated body, fights with a full moveset, transforms and casts
an ultimate, and is covered by the automated gates. **None** is production-ready against a
commercial fighting game: the bodies are stylised lofted geometry with no image textures and the
clips are scripted rather than captured. The three identities whose licensed source packages are
still missing use this project's generated bodies and are labelled accordingly.

### Impact readability pass (this revision)

The complaints this pass started from were that a hit buries the fighters in a white puff and that
specials pull the camera too far back. Both are measured, not eyeballed:
`bash scripts/run-browser-test.sh scripts/visual-gallery.mjs` captures the real game through its own
camera at one fixed viewport, zoom and arena, and reports the largest live effect near a fighter as
a ratio against that fighter's projected height (a ratio above 1 means the effect is taller on
screen than the fighter it covers).

| Capture (Goku) | effect/fighter ratio before | ratio after | effect meshes before | after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| idle | 0.26 | 0.51 | 126 | 84 |
| light attack | 0.71 | 0.65 | 275 | 80 |
| signature special | 0.66 | 0.55 | 79 | 79 |
| transformation | 0.30 | 0.19 | 84 | 39 |
| targeted impact, intensity 23 (the case that buried a fighter) | 0.69 | 0.61 | 71 | 45 |

The cause was in `public/js/combat_vfx.js`: one shared formula gave every hit a flash whose radius
grew with the attack intensity and was then multiplied by up to 4.2x during its life, so a
23-intensity explosion built a ~2 m additive sphere at .98 opacity sitting on the target. The
flash is now graded by hit kind (`light` / `heavy` / `guard` / `ultimate`) with a hard radius
ceiling, a short life, thin expanding rings and directional sparks, and it is no longer scaled
after creation. Gameplay is untouched: hit radius, damage, hitstun and hit detection are still the
combat system's, and the effect only reads the intensity it is handed.

Before/after frames are in `artifacts/gallery/before2`, `artifacts/gallery/after` and
`artifacts/gallery/after-ironspider`; the form pass for the contrasting fighters is in
`artifacts/gallery/forms-hulk` (Thunderclap, worst 0.61) and `artifacts/gallery/forms-kaze`
(WIND-STEP, worst 0.60). Every one of those frames was opened and inspected: the before frame
shows the fighter buried under an opaque white mass with the head and torso unreadable, and the
same shot after the change shows him fully visible with a small contact spark and thin rings.

Camera framing was measured separately on the real controller with
`bash scripts/run-browser-test.sh scripts/measure-camera.mjs` (14 samples, two arenas, the test
moves the fighters itself rather than clicking hidden UI): **0 fighters out of frame**, specials at
fov 39.5-42.6 instead of a distant pull-back, the smallest fighter height 104 px in the widest
sample, and no measured case that justified an attack-specific camera limit. Nothing in the camera
or the render pipeline was changed - `public/js/smash_render_pipeline.js` is untouched.

## 8. Remaining work, highest priority first

1. **Animation.** The single largest gap: 17 authored clips per body and a 39-clip procedural
   library, no capture. Licensed clips (with licence recorded) or hand-authored Blender clips,
   retargeted through `public/js/anim/rig_map.js`, would move `animation` from 2 to 4+ across the
   whole roster at once.
2. **Materials.** No character image texture exists in the repository. Authored albedo/normal/ORM
   at 1–2k per body family would lift `materials` and `face`, and is a purely original-work task.
3. **Sculpted anatomy and faces.** Lofted bodies cannot reach sculpted quality; the next step is a
   sculpted/scanned base mesh per body archetype, skinned with soft weights. The one lever that
   worked without new source art is per-identity detail geometry: `build_signature_details()` in
   `assets/blender/build_roster_fighters.py` now adds Goku's forward fringe, sideburns and forearm
   wraps, and Iron Man's emissive palm repulsors and chest vents, to the shared anatomy.
4. **Per-character move behaviour.** Signature moves currently share behaviour classes
   (`rush`/`storm`/`zone`/`beam`/`grab`/`slam`/`barrage`). One bespoke behaviour per identity
   would raise `signature` from 3 to 5.
5. **Supplied licensed models.** Goku, Iron Man and Iron Spider are blocked on source packages;
   `scripts/install-character-assets.mjs` and `scripts/install-character-model.mjs` are the tested
   route in, and nothing else has to change.
6. **Foot IK and per-character clip sets** to remove the remaining foot sliding, and LODs plus a
   GPU-measured performance budget on a real Chromebook.
